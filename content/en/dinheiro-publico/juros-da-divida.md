## What is it?

It is the interest the government pays on what it owes, added up over the
last 12 months and shown as a share of GDP, the value of everything the
country produces in a year.

## Why does it matter?

Every real spent on interest is a real that does not go to schools, health
or roads. When interest weighs a lot, it is harder to stop the debt from
growing.

The interest the government pays rises and falls with the basic interest
rate, the Selic. In 2020, with the Selic at 2% a year, interest on the debt
was low; when the Selic rises, it rises too.

## How is it calculated?

The Central Bank publishes the total result of the government's accounts
(the nominal result, with interest) and the result without interest (the
primary result). Brasil Fácil does the sum: total minus primary equals
interest. It is the Central Bank's own definition.

For example, in the 12 months to December 2020, the total deficit was 13.34%
of GDP and the primary deficit 9.24%. The difference, 4.10% of GDP, was
interest.

It covers the whole public sector: federal government, Central Bank, INSS,
states, cities and state companies (except Petrobras and Eletrobras).

This page does not show the Good, Watch or Worrying colours.

## How to read the chart?

The purple line is the interest paid over 12 months, in % of GDP. The higher
it is, the more of what the country produces goes to interest on the debt.
The line usually rises a few months after the Selic goes up.

## Where does it come from?

From the Central Bank, every month: nominal result (series 5727) minus
primary result (series 5793). The technical name is "juros nominais do setor
público consolidado". The series starts in November 2002.
