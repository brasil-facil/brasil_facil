## What is it?

The minimum wage is the lowest amount a worker with a formal job can be paid
per month. It is set by law and changes once a year, in January.

## Why does it matter?

Many people earn exactly the minimum wage. It is also the lowest amount of
INSS pensions and of benefits such as the BPC. So when the minimum wage goes
up, the income of millions of families goes up too, and so does government
spending.

## How is it calculated?

Under the current rule, each year's minimum wage is the previous year's plus
two parts:

1. inflation for lower-income families (INPC) over the 12 months to November;
2. the growth of the economy (GDP) two years earlier. Since 2025, this part
   is capped at 2.5% a year.

For the chart, Brasil Fácil adjusts each older value for inflation (INPC) up
to the latest month. For example, the R$ 64.79 of July 1994 would be worth
about R$ 551 today. So if the line goes up, the minimum wage buys more
things, not just a bigger number.

This page does not show the Good, Watch or Worrying colours. There is no
official target, and a higher or lower minimum wage is not, on its own, good
or bad: it raises the income of those who earn it, but also the costs of
companies and the government.

## How to read the chart?

The purple line is the minimum wage, always in today's reais. It moves in
steps: it rises in January, when the new value starts, and slips a little
during the year, because prices rise and the same amount buys less. The chart
starts in July 1994, when the real became Brazil's currency.

## Where does it come from?

Each month's value comes from the Banco Central (series 1619). The inflation
used to adjust the values is the INPC, from IBGE.
