## What is it?

The real interest rate is the Selic minus expected inflation. If the Selic is
14% and expected inflation is 5%, the real rate is close to 9%. It shows how
much money really earns or costs once prices go up.

## Why does it matter?

People who lend or save money look at the real rate. A high real rate slows
down spending and investment and helps hold prices back. A low real rate
stimulates the economy.

## How to read the chart?

The purple line is the real interest rate. The grey line is the Selic. The gap
between them is the inflation the market expects. We do not show a colour for
the real rate yet: we are gathering the Central Bank's estimates of the
neutral rate, the level that neither slows down nor stimulates the economy.

## Where does it come from?

From the Central Bank. The Selic comes from the Copom's decisions. Expected
inflation comes from the Focus survey, a weekly poll of economists at banks
and companies.
