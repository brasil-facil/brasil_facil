## What is it?

It is how much working people receive per month, adding up all their jobs:
formal, informal and self-employed. It covers people aged 14 or over who
worked in the survey week.

## Why does it matter?

Income from work is the main source of money for most families. Looking only
at the average hides a lot: a few people who earn a great deal pull the
average up. That is why we show the median (the person in the middle) and the
two ends.

## How is it calculated?

IBGE asks tens of thousands of families how much each person usually
receives per month. Then it lines up all the incomes, from lowest to highest,
and notes a few points in the line:

- **10% earn less than**: the amount below which are the 10% who earn least;
- **Half earn less than**: the person right in the middle of the line (the
  median);
- **10% earn more than**: the amount above which are the 10% who earn most.

IBGE publishes these amounts in each year's reais. Brasil Fácil adjusts each
year for inflation (INPC) up to the latest month, to compare what income
bought at each time.

This page does not show the Good, Watch or Worrying colours: there is no
official target for income.

## How to read the chart?

The purple line is the median: half of people earn less than it. The band
shows where the middle 80% are. The dashed line is the minimum wage, in
today's reais. It sits between the bottom of the band and the median: many
people earn close to the minimum wage, and the bottom 10% earn much less than
it, which is common among people who work informally or few hours. "See the
numbers" has the values for each year.

The top 1% does not fit in the chart: in 2025, those people earned more than
R$ 24,839 a month, in that year's reais.

## Where does it come from?

From IBGE, through the PNAD Contínua, in its yearly edition on income (table
7536). The data starts in 2012. The inflation used to adjust the values is
the INPC, also from IBGE.
