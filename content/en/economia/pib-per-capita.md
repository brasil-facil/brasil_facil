## What is it?

GDP is the value of everything a country produces in a year: food, cars,
houses, services. GDP per capita divides that value by the number of people.
It shows how much wealth the country produces, on average, per person.

## Why does it matter?

A higher GDP per person usually comes with higher wages and more money for
schools, health and roads. But it is an average: it does not show how income
is shared between people.

## How is it calculated?

1. GDP adds up the value of everything the country produced in the year. In
   Brazil, IBGE calculates it.
2. The IMF turns the values into dollars without using the day's exchange
   rate. It compares how much the same basket of goods costs in each country
   (purchasing power parity, PPP).
3. It removes the effect of inflation, bringing everything to 2021 prices.
4. It divides by the number of people.

The IMF calculates one number per year, so the chart has one point per year.

This page does not show the Good, Watch or Worrying colours: there is no
official target for GDP per person.

## How to read the chart?

The purple line is Brazil. The grey lines are other countries, and the solid
grey line is the average of advanced economies, the richest countries. The
values are in dollars adjusted to the cost of living in each country (what
economists call PPP). So US$ 100 buys about the same amount of things in every
country. And they are in 2021 dollars, without the effect of inflation: if the line goes up, production per person really grew.

These are not market-rate dollars. To see how expensive Brazil is in
market-rate dollars, see the [Price level](/en/economy/price-level/) page.

## Where does it come from?

From the IMF, the International Monetary Fund, which gathers each country's
official figures twice a year. We show only years with published data, not
the IMF's forecasts.
