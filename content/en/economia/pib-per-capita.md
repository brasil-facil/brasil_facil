## What is it?

GDP is the value of everything a country produces in a year: food, cars,
houses, services. GDP per capita divides that value by the number of people.
It shows how much wealth the country produces, on average, per person.

## Why does it matter?

A higher GDP per person usually comes with higher wages and more money for
schools, health and roads. But it is an average: it does not show how income
is shared between people.

## How to read the chart?

The purple line is Brazil. The grey lines are other countries, and the solid
grey line is the average of advanced economies, the richest countries. The
values are in dollars adjusted to the cost of living in each country (what
economists call PPP). So US$ 100 buys about the same amount of things in every
country. And they are in 2021 dollars, without the effect of inflation: if the
line goes up, production per person really grew.

## Where does it come from?

From the IMF, the International Monetary Fund, which gathers each country's
official figures twice a year. We show only years with published data, not
the IMF's forecasts.
