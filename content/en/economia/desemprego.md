## What is it?

Unemployment counts the people who did not work, were ready to start and
looked for a job in the last month. The rate shows how many they are, out of
every 100 people who want to work. People who are not looking for work, like
students and retirees, are not counted.

## Why does it matter?

When unemployment rises, more families lose income, and people with jobs tend
to get smaller pay rises. A fast rise often shows up when the economy is
slowing down.

## How to read the chart?

The purple line is the unemployment rate. It goes up at the start of every
year, when temporary end-of-year jobs end. That is why the colour compares
each period with the same period a year earlier. Green: it rose less than 0.2
point or fell. Yellow: it rose 0.2 to 0.5 point. Red: it rose 0.5 point or
more.

The grey lines are other countries, calculated by the OECD with the same
definition of unemployment. There are small differences: in Brazil, the
survey counts people aged 14 and over and does not remove the effect of the
seasons. For the other countries, the OECD counts people aged 15 and over and
removes that effect. So compare the direction of the lines more than the
exact number. South Africa is not shown because it does not publish
unemployment every month.

## Where does it come from?

From IBGE, Brazil's statistics office, every month, through the PNAD Contínua
survey. The technical name is "taxa de desocupação". The survey started in
2012, so the chart starts that year.
